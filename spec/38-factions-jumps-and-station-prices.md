## 38. Factions, jumps and station prices

The vanilla factions take sides in the chase. Hot zones pay for their danger, like the hazard beacons of FTL, so a route through them is a real choice. Code: `src/HotCargo/Factions`. Strings: `mod/factions`. Numbers: the `Factions` tunables section. Pure math: `Factions/FactionsModel.cs`.

The trade-off, with the default values:

| Route | Cost | Pay |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate ally space (hot zone) | Time heat ×1.5. No shelter. Local ships do not fight the hunters and take no hire contract. | Offers pay ×1.3 money. Notoriety ×1.5, so the tech value and the Buyer score grow faster. |
| Rival space | — | Fences pay ×1.5 for tech and never refuse. |
| Space of a faction that likes you | You need 500 reputation with it. | Time heat ×0.6 (shelter). Its stations never refuse. |
| Relay with no hunter near it | Get to the relay before the hunters do. | The jump cuts 85 % of the heat (a watched relay cuts 75 %). |
| Blind jump | ×1.5 fuel, ×2 spin-up, a 35 % chance per ship of part damage on arrival. | The jump cuts 90 % of the heat. |

640. **Roles.** Each run, one vanilla faction is the corporation's ally and another one is its rival. `AllyFaction` and `RivalFaction` name a faction ID; an empty value picks one from the galaxy seed (`GameInit.RandSeed`). The pick is a pure function of the seed and the faction list, so it needs no save state and every co-op machine gets the same roles. The two roles always differ.
641. **Hot zone.** A system whose controlling faction (`MapNode.ControllingFaction`, the faction with the most strength) is the ally is corporate ally space. Its time heat (requirement 24) × `AllyHeatFactor` (1.5).
642. **Hot zone pay.** A mission money reward × `AllyMoneyFactor` (1.3) when the mission was offered in a hot zone (`Mission.SpawnedAt`). The patch is on the vanilla money reward (`MoneyReward.GetScaledMoney`), so the reward text, the sort value and the payout agree. The contract bonus (requirement 102) does not change. Fame does not change, so the heat of a mission does not change. Notoriety gains in a hot zone × `AllyNotorietyFactor` (1.5): the tech value (requirement 19) and with it the Buyer score grow faster.
643. **Shelter.** A controlling faction that is not the ally shelters the players while their reputation with it is `ShelterReputation` (500, vanilla Friend) or more. In its systems, time heat × `ShelterHeatFactor` (0.6).
644. **Fences.** Stations and trade ships of the rival faction (its player indexes) pay the tech value × `RivalFencePremium` (1.5). Buy-back of tech there costs the same factor more, so a sell and buy-back loop still loses money. The Buyer (Neutral index) never changes.
645. **Where the player decides.** The galaxy map tooltip of a system shows its zone: corporate ally space with its factors, shelter, or the reputation that shelter needs (and the current one), and "Fences" in rival space. The label under the system name shows HOT ZONE (ally) or FENCES (rival). On arrival in a hot zone, rival space or a shelter, a chat line names it.
650. **Jumps cut heat.** Vanilla has one way to change system: the ships fly to a Hyper-Jump Relay, spin up their hyperdrives and pay hyperium. Every jump removes a share of the heat, and the run keeps the rest (requirement 28). A relay jump removes `RelayWatchedHeatCut` (0.75) when a hunter (live or in stasis) is within `RelayWatchRadius` (3,500) of that relay when the jump starts, else `RelayClearHeatCut` (0.85): the corporation watches the relays. Blockade ships (requirement 916) park inside this radius, so a blockaded relay is a watched relay. A blind jump removes `BlindHeatCut` (0.9). The kind is fixed when the jump starts and charges its fuel (`GameRoot.DoHumanPlayersFtlJump`), from sim state only, so a later click does not change it. Any player sets the blind switch through the MP input `HotCargoBlindJump`. The run state saves the switch. It turns off after a blind jump, so it holds for one jump. A relay lockdown (requirement 950) blocks relay jumps, never blind jumps.
651. **Blind jump costs.** With the blind switch on: the map jump fuel of each human ship × `BlindFuelFactor` (1.5), rounded up (`FtlManager.GetTotalFtlMapJumpFuelCost`, the one source of the fuel labels, the spin-up check and the charge). A ship is ready only after `BlindSpinUpFactor` (2) × its vanilla spin-up time: the run state counts the spin-up time of each ship and notes when its drives are full, and a drive that goes off (damage, no power, no fuel) restarts the count. Turning the switch on restarts every spin-up. On arrival, each ship that jumped rolls `BlindDamageChance` (0.35), seeded by the ship and the sector index; a hit takes `BlindDamageFraction` (0.4) of the maximum health from `BlindDamageParts` (3) random parts, never below 1 health. A toast and a chat line name the damaged ships.
652. **Jump UI.** The spin-up window shows a button under ENGAGE with the heat cut of the jump that would start now: "Relay jump: heat -85%", "Watched relay: heat -75%" (red) or "BLIND jump: heat -90%". Its tooltip lists the three cuts and the blind risks (fuel, spin-up, damage chance). The galaxy map tooltip of a system in jump range shows the heat on arrival by a clear relay, a watched relay and a blind jump, and says when hunters are near that relay now. The run intro names the three cuts.
653. **Jumps in combat.** Vanilla has no rule against a jump near enemies, but it drops a jump while game saving is blocked: multi-step explosions, self-destructing parts, jump-in invulnerability and some spawns hold a save block, because the system switch saves the current system. In a fight that is most of the time, so ENGAGE greys out and a jump that all players readied does not start. Hot Cargo keeps ENGAGE active, and a jump that meets a save block waits until it clears (like the vanilla `MPJumpToNodeInput`), then starts. The risk of a jump in a fight is the spin-up under fire: the ship must hold at the relay while its drives fill, and a hit that knocks a drive out restarts the spin-up.
655. **Station prices by heat.** From `MarkupStartHeat` (75) heat, what stations and trade ships of a faction charge × a factor that rises in a straight line to `MarkupMaxFactor` (2) at heat 100: resources (fuel included), tech buy-back, the resources that construction buys there, and blueprints. Heat counts in whole steps of `PriceHeatStep` (5), so a price holds between steps. Sale refunds do not change. Limit: a resource trade carries its price in the MP input, and the sim drops a trade whose price changed (vanilla `MPMoveResourcesInput`), so a click in the moment that heat crosses a step can be lost.
656. **Crew price.** The crew hire price × a factor that rises in a straight line from 1 at heat 0 to `CrewPriceAtMaxHeat` (2) at heat 100, in the same heat steps, rounded up to the vanilla hire price multiple. The hire tab shows the price and a line "Heat: crew cost +N%".
657. **Refusals.** From `RefuseHeat` (90) heat, each station (trade and military) refuses with chance `RefuseChance` (0.5). The roll is seeded by the station and the sector index, so it holds for the visit. A station that refuses does not answer a hail, an open comms window with it closes, and crew cannot trade with it. Fences (rival stations), the Buyer, stations of a faction that shelters the players, a station that collects a relay toll (requirement 956) and a station that owes the caller a mission reward never refuse.
658. **Refusal message.** A hail to a station that refuses shows a toast and a chat line: "<station> refuses to talk: "You are too hot. Go away."". Each time heat rises into the markup band or the refusal band (for example after a jump lowered it), a chat line says so. The refusal band also shows a toast; the markup band does not, because the Hunt stage alert starts at the same heat by default.
659. **Comms badges.** The comms list shows badges before the service badges, because the row cuts long text: FENCE ×N tech, SHELTER, CORPORATE ALLY, REFUSES and PRICES ×N.
660. **Map markers.** With `MapMarkers` on, the galaxy map draws the vanilla custom marker icon (`CareerMode.CustomMarkerIcon`, custom_marker.png) at the upper right of each system that holds a target, and its tooltip lists the targets by name. Targets: accepted contracts (missions with terms, requirement 100) and Hot Cargo missions (event, chain and Buyer missions, metatype IDs that start with "HotCargo") at the system of their nearest objective, and story chains that wait at their station's system (a station offer or a return stage). Vanilla already draws its mission icon on the systems of accepted missions; the vanilla custom marker is a point in the current system, not on the galaxy map, so the mod draws the icon itself. The markers are UI only and add no save state.
661. **Determinism.** Every rule reads sim state only: heat, the sector index, reputation, map node factions, the galaxy seed, the station ID, the hunters and the run tunables. The prices are patched where both the UI and the MP inputs read them. The blind switch changes only through its MP input and the system switch. All effects stop after a win (heat off). The blind spin-up counts are sim state in the run save. The wait for a save block (requirement 653) checks the block once per tick, like the vanilla view switch (`MPJumpToNodeInput`).
662. **Tests.** Headless: `factions-zones` (hot zone heat, money and notoriety, shelter, fence price, markers), `factions-prices` (markup at 75, 90 and 100 heat, crew price, refusals and the message), `factions-jumps` (clear relay, watched relay and blind heat cuts, blind fuel, blind part damage), `factions-combat-jump` (the blind spin-up time, and a jump that waits for a save block in a fight and then starts), `factions-peace` (requirements 1150 and 1151 in ally space, and the vanilla fight and hire in neutral space). Co-op: steps `factions.prices`, `factions.blind` and `factions.peace` in `coop-economy` (a fence sale and a marked-up buy pass the exact price check, the blind switch, and the peace in ally space after a fight in neutral space, with no desync). Unit tests: `FactionsModelTests`.
1150. **Peace in ally space.** In corporate ally space (requirement 641), the ally faction's ships and the hunters are not enemies. This covers all ships with the ally's military and civilian player indexes: patrols, police, stations and traders. Neither side targets or damages the other, and the faction does not join a fight between the players and the hunters. The relation of the faction to the players does not change. In neutral space and in shelter, the vanilla rule holds: hunters are enemies of every faction (requirement 33), so other factions fight them. The zone of the current system decides, so a hunter that follows the players to another system gets the rule of that system. The patch is on `CareerSimModeManager.AreEnemies`, which AI targeting, auto-fire and damage (`SimModeManager.CanDamage`) read. Each sim tick stores the zone, the ally's player indexes and the shared player index of the live hunters from sim state, so every co-op machine agrees. When the peace starts or a new hunter side arrives, both sides drop the attack orders and turret targets that they have on each other, like the vanilla truce. Limit: a hunter that leaves stasis is covered from the next tick.
1151. **No help in ally space.** In corporate ally space, the ally faction's ships take no hire or mercenary contract (requirements 579 and 583), because they do not fight the hunters there. Its military ships also never hold tech (requirement 574), so they have no option for the players and do not answer calls (requirement 560).
