## 45. Autonomous ships

Ships that run only on their ship AI rows (section 30). The player builds them, writes their rows and sets their part settings, but cannot give them orders. Code: `src/HotCargo/Autonomy`, pure rules in `AutonomyModel.cs`. Part rules: `mod/autonomy/`. Strings: `mod/autonomy/strings.en.rules`. Tunables: the `Autonomy` section. The new conditions and actions extend `ShipAiModel` (`src/HotCargo/ShipAi`).

### Terms

- **Autonomous ship**: a human player ship with at least one Computer part and no part of another kind that gives command points (a control room). A ship with a Computer and a control room is a normal ship: the Computer only adds command points and mans its part.
- **Cargo**: the resources in the flexible storage grids of the ship (vanilla storage bays). Ammo, battery and tech storages are not cargo.
- **Loot**: a resource item in space (a vanilla nugget).
- **Trading station**: a ship that trades with the player ship (`ShipAI.WillTradeWith`), a station or a vanilla trade ship.
- **Crewed ship**: a human player ship with a working control room.

### Parts

1050. **Computer**, ID `yarden_zamir.computer`, 2×1, rotatable, `EditorGroup = "Control"`, solid (crew cannot walk through it). It gives command points and sensors like `control_room_small` (`CommandProvider` 50 points; `Sensor` sight 400, radar 1,800). It needs no crew. It needs power: it stores battery and drains 1 power every 60 s like `control_room_small`. It works (is operational) while it has power, its on/off switch is on and it is not scorched. Cost: 16 steel, 40 coil, 4 processors. Health: 2,000 (half of `control_room_small` for half the tiles).
1051. **Computer mans one part.** A working Computer mans exactly one part: the part that touches its front side (the long side that its arrow points to). When two parts touch the front side, the part at the left front tile (seen along the arrow) wins. Only a part with a vanilla crew slot (`PartCrew`) that gives no command points can be manned. A manned part counts as fully crewed: each of its `PartCrew` components is on, and vanilla crew do not go there. A part with crew of its own and a Computer counts as manned. Two Computers can face the same part; it is manned while one of them works. Code: a postfix on `PartCrew.IsToggleOn` and `IsToggleOnAnticipated`, a postfix on `PartCrewJob.DesiredCrew` (0 for a manned part), and the vanilla `ToggleChanged` event when the manned state changes.
1052. **The trade-off**: a Computer mans one part with 2 tiles, whatever the crew count of that part. Crew quarters with the same 2 tiles hold 2 crew (`crew_quarters_small`), who walk, repair, build, carry power and man any part. Limit: a Computer that faces a 4-crew part (a cannon deck) gives more crew work than 2 tiles of quarters. Revisit after playtests if Computer ships with big crewed weapons beat crewed ships of the same cost.
1053. **Targeted reactor**, ID `yarden_zamir.targeted_reactor`, 2×2, not rotatable, `EditorGroup = "Power"`. It makes battery at half the rate of `reactor_small`: 750 battery per second (500 every 2/3 s). It compares to `reactor_small` because both are 2×2: the same tiles give half the power, and in return need no crew and no corridor. It puts the battery straight into the `BatteryStorage` of each part that touches one of its sides, with vanilla `ResourceStorageProxy` components (one per outside edge tile, as `thruster_rocket_battery` does). It never feeds a part that provides power (reactors, power storage), so crew cannot carry its power on. It has no storage that crew can take battery from. It is solid: crew cannot walk through it (`CrewSpeedFactor = 0`, no door places). Overclock and electron debuffs work as on `reactor_small`. Cost: 32 steel, 60 coil, 4 enriched uranium.
1054. **Funnel**, ID `yarden_zamir.funnel`, 2×2, `EditorGroup = "Storage"`. It needs no crew and no power. Every `TickSeconds` of game time (default 0.25 s, on sim ticks that are a multiple of it, so a late co-op client runs it on the same ticks), it takes in each loot item that its filter accepts (requirement 1055) and that lies inside its area: its own tiles grown by `FunnelReach` tiles on each side (default 1, so a 4×4 area). It moves the item into the parts that touch its sides: storage bay tiles and any typed storage tile that accepts the resource and has room (`IResourceSink.GetRemainingCapacity`, minus deliveries that crew already bring). Parts go in order of the touching tile (top, right, bottom, left, each from its start). What finds no room stays in space. Tech crates follow requirement 14: only Tech Vaults take tech. The Funnel is walled and crew can walk through it. Cost: 30 steel, 10 coil.
1055. **Funnel filter**: a vanilla part setting (a popout `UIToggle`, like the missile launcher ammo choice) with the choices: ship loot filter (the default, requirement 1062), all loot, unit value at least 50, at least 200, at least 1,000, off. Unit value is the vanilla sell price of one unit (`GetResourceSellRefund`). Vanilla part settings sync in co-op and save with the ship and the design, so the filter needs no code of its own for that.
1056. **Manipulator beam**: a Computer can man a vanilla manipulator beam. The "collect loot" action (requirement 1068) gives the vanilla collect order for loot in beam range, so the beam pulls loot to the ship from far away. The Funnel is the short-range option without crew and power.
1057. **Art**: each part has its own sprites in `mod/autonomy/sprites/<part>/`, made by `tools/sprites/make_sprites.py` (run it with `uv run`; the PNG files are its output, and the script is the source). They follow the vanilla terran conventions, with no vanilla art copied: 64 px per tile; the layers floor, walls, roof (green paint mask) with a normal map made from the same height map, doodads, lights, blueprints and the editor icon; damage levels `_33` and `_66` with scorch marks. The Computer is a server cabinet with a screen strip at its front, the side it serves. The targeted reactor is a sealed core with an emitter toward each side; its rules replace the vanilla `reactor_small` visual components by name (`Graphics`, the reactor lights, the battery sprites). The Funnel is an open ringed intake with turning vanes and no roof. Screenshots: `tests/screens/autonomy-sprites.txt` (roofs, build mode, the part list, damage levels).

### Control

1058. **No orders.** An autonomous ship takes no move, attack or weapon target orders from any player. The sim refuses them: `MPEnqueueCommandsInput`, `MPReplaceCommandInput` and the weapon target inputs drop each command or target for an autonomous ship. The UI leaves autonomous ships out of `PlayerInputHandler.SelectedCommandExecutableShips`, so a right click shows no order line and the halt button does not act on them. The player can still select the ship, build on it, change part settings and open the AI editor.
1059. **The ship stays the player's.** An autonomous ship with a working Computer is significant (`SimModeManager.IsShipSignificant`), and losing a part, its crew or a split does not make it a wreck (`CareerSimModeManager.GetAllegianceForReducedShip` keeps the owner). Without a working Computer (destroyed, no power, scorched) the vanilla rules apply again: a ship without crew and command then becomes a wreck on the next loss, as in vanilla.
1060. **Building and repair.** A ship without crew cannot build or repair itself. Crew of an own crewed ship nearby build and repair it with the vanilla helper construction (`SimModeManager.CanShipHelpWithConstruction`). A new autonomous ship starts like any new ship (`AllowStartNewShip`), next to a crewed ship.
1061. **AI.** The rows of section 30 run on autonomous ships the same way. They never wait for a player order (requirement 408), because there are none. A new autonomous ship starts with Auto on and no rows; it holds still until the player adds rows.

### Ship settings

1062. **Cargo settings**: each ship has four lists next to its rows: the loot filter (a minimum unit value, or a list of resource types), the sell list, the mine list (ore types) and the buy list (resource types). An empty sell list sells all cargo. An empty mine list mines any ore. An empty buy list buys nothing. The editor (requirement 410) shows them in a "Cargo" block: a drop list for the loot filter mode and value, and a "Types" button for each list that opens a check list of the resource types. The mine list offers the resources that asteroid deposit parts give (from the asteroid ship rules). The other lists offer every stackable resource except tech.
1063. **Cargo text**: `HCC1:` then `loot=v<value>` or `loot=<id>,<id>`, then `sell=`, `mine=` and `buy=` with resource IDs, joined by `|`. Copy and paste of the editor carry the rows and the cargo text on two lines. An unknown resource ID is refused with a message, like bad rows (requirement 405).
1064. **Return to me**: a switch in the editor's cargo block. While it is on, the ship ignores its move rows and follows the owner's first crewed ship (requirement 1071). Target, weapons and facing rows still run. It is the one way to recall an autonomous ship.
1065. The cargo settings and the return switch travel with the rows: the tactics input (requirement 409) carries them, the design and the career save store them (requirement 407), and they are part of the ship's integrity hash.

### New conditions

1066. Conditions (they extend requirement 401):
   - **Cargo above X %**: cargo over cargo capacity. "Storage full" is cargo above 95 %. A ship without storage bays never matches.
   - **Cargo to sell**: the cargo holds a resource of the sell list.
   - **Loot within R**: a loot item that the loot filter accepts, within R (edge distance from the hull) and in sight of the team.
   - **Station in hail range**: a trading station is within the vanilla trade distance (`CareerSimModeManager.AllowResourceTrade`: hull circles within `LoseHomeShipWarningDistance`, 400). Trade needs this distance, so it stands for "hail distance".
   - **Asteroid within R**: an asteroid with ore of the mine list within R (requirement 1070).
   - **Wreck within R**: a wreck (a junk ship that is not an asteroid and has parts) within R and on radar.
   - **Fleet beyond R**: the owner's nearest crewed ship is further than R, edge to edge, or the owner has no crewed ship in this system.
   Distances are threshold terms with hysteresis (requirement 418).

### New actions

1067. Actions (they extend requirement 402). Move: collect loot, go to station, mine, salvage, return to fleet. A new kind, **Trade**: sell, buy, unload. The first matching trade row runs each AI tick, as with the other kinds.
1068. **Collect loot**: the ship flies so that its nearest Funnel passes over the nearest loot that the loot filter accepts (with no Funnel, its center). It also gives the vanilla collect order (`ManualResourceTransfer`, as requirement 414) for each accepted loot item within the crew safe distance, so crew and manned manipulator beams fetch it. A ship without crew and without a manned manipulator beam gives no collect order, because the open jobs would hold room in the bays that the Funnel needs. It can run while accepted loot is in sight and the cargo has room.
1069. **Go to station**, parameter: nearest, best price (pays most for the cargo of the sell list, ties to the nearest) or cheapest (sells the buy list for least, ties to the nearest). The ship flies to the spot `DockDistance` (default 150) from the station hull on its own side, inside the trade distance, and holds there. A station in stasis gets a move order to its place; it turns live as the ship comes near. It can run while the system has a trading station that is not an enemy.
1070. **Mine**: the ship flies to the nearest asteroid with ore of the mine list, physical or in stasis, and holds at `MineDistance` (default 80, edge to edge) with its nose toward the asteroid (the move order's rotation), so lasers at the nose reach it. Within 3 × `MineDistance` it gives the vanilla salvage order (`ResourceManager.SalvagePart`) for the deposit parts with ore of the mine list nearest to the ship, with that list as the collect types, and keeps at most `SalvageJobs` (default 4) such orders open. Vanilla mining lasers pick these jobs (`TurretWeapon.TryAcquireSalvageTarget`); the ore drops as loot, which collect loot and Funnels take in. It can run while such an asteroid exists and the cargo has room. The ore of a deposit part is its `Rules.Resources`. Limit: the mine order uses its own collect list, not the vanilla banned types, because `ShipMetadata.IsResourceTypeBanned` is not deterministic.
1071. **Return to fleet**: the ship follows the owner's first crewed ship (lowest ID). With none in the system it holds still. "Follow my first ship" (requirement 402) and guard "my first ship" use the first crewed ship too when the ship is autonomous.
1072. **Salvage**: like mine, on the nearest wreck on radar, for every part. It can run while a wreck is on radar.
1073. **Sell**: while a trading station is in hail range, the ship sells all cargo of the sell list to the nearest such station. Each unit goes at once from the ship's storage bays to the station's storage (`ResourceManager.AddResources` on the station; only the units it takes leave the bays), and the owner gets the vanilla sell price for it (`GetResourceSellRefund` for the station's faction) through `PlayerState.AddMoney`. Units that the station has no room for stay. No crew carry anything, so it also works without crew. Limit: delivery missions do not count these units, because vanilla credits a delivery only when crew deliver. Revisit if players use autonomous ships for delivery missions.
1074. **Buy**: the same at once, the other way, from the station in hail range: the ship buys resources of the buy list from the station's stock until the cargo is full, at the vanilla buy cost (`GetResourceBuyCost`), and never spends below `BuyReserve` (default 5,000) of the owner's money.
1075. All new actions read and change only sim state and run in the AI tick (requirement 411). Stations, asteroids and wrecks are picked by distance, ties by object ID. Stasis asteroids come from `sim.Stasis` sorted by distance and reserved ID.

### Scenarios

1076. **Looter** (preset "Looter"): `CargoAbove=95 → GoToStation=nearest`; `StationInRange & CargoToSell → Sell`; `LootWithin=3000 → CollectLoot`; `Always → ReturnToFleet`. Ship: Computer, Funnel, storage bay, targeted reactor, thrusters. The loot filter picks the loot (value or types), the sell list says what to sell.
1077. **Miner** (preset "Miner"): `CargoAbove=95 → GoToStation=nearest`; `StationInRange & CargoToSell → Sell`; `LootWithin=600 → CollectLoot`; `AsteroidWithin=20000 → Mine`; `Always → ReturnToFleet`. Ship: Computer facing a mining laser, targeted reactor next to both, Funnel, storage bay, thrusters. The mine list picks the asteroid types.
1078. **Supply runner** (preset "Supply runner"): `CargoAbove=90 → ReturnToFleet`; `Always → GoToStation=cheapest`; `!FleetBeyond=300 → Unload`; `StationInRange → Buy`. It buys the buy list where it is cheapest and unloads all cargo into the owner's nearest crewed ship. **Unload** (trade kind): while an own crewed ship is within the vanilla trade distance, all cargo goes at once into that ship's storages that take it (`ResourceManager.AddResources`). A miner can unload ore for the fleet's factories the same way. A hauler between two stations is not a preset: vanilla buy prices are about twice the sell refund (`EconDifficulty.ResourceSaleRefund` 50 %), and Hot Cargo only raises buy prices (requirement 655), so such a route always loses money.
1079. **Salvager** (preset "Salvager"): the looter rows with `WreckWithin=3000 → Salvage` above the return row. Ship: a Computer facing a mining laser.
1080. **Escort** (preset "Escort drone"): `HealthBelow=40 → ReturnToFleet`; `AllyUnderAttack → TargetAllyAttacker`; `EnemyWithin=1500 → TargetNearest`; `Always → Guard=my first ship`. Ship: a Computer facing each weapon it needs (one Computer per weapon), targeted reactors. A guard row of an autonomous ship guards the first crewed ship.
1081. **Return when damaged**: any preset can start with `HealthBelow=X → ReturnToFleet`; crew of the crewed ship repair it there (requirement 1060).
1082. Each scenario has an autotest that builds the ship in code from a part list (`auto_build`, no ship files) and runs without teleports after the start: `autonomy-looter`, `autonomy-miner`, `autonomy-supply`, `autonomy-salvager`, `autonomy-escort`. `autonomy-basic` checks manning, the targeted reactor refill, the refused order, "return to me" and the ownership rules. The co-op session `mp:coop-autonomy` (`tests/autotest-mp/coop-autonomy.txt`, steps `autonomy.looter` and `autonomy.miner`) runs a looter and a miner of the host in its own run, near the start station and its asteroids. The client checks the sale money, the manned laser and the cargo on its own machine, and the session state hash compares both machines. Unit tests cover the model rules: manning pick, Funnel area, filters, cargo text, station and asteroid pick order, sell and buy amounts.

Requirements 1083 to 1099 stay free for this section.
