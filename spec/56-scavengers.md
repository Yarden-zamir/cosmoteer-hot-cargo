## 56. Scavengers

Ships pick the system clean of old wrecks and loose resources, and sell what they find. The player always gets the first pick: a scavenger never takes anything fresh. Code: `src/HotCargo/Scavenge/`. Numbers: the `Scavenge` tunables section. Pure rules: `Scavenge/ScavengeModel.cs`. Requirements 1330 to 1359.

### The shared behavior

1330. **What counts.** A scavenger takes two kinds of targets. A wreck is a junk piece (`Ship.IsJunk`) with parts that is not an asteroid, a station or landmark (a special ship component), the relic (`hc_relic_ship`) or a vanilla abandoned ship (label `abandoned`). It has no crew aboard, no mission names it (`Mission.IsShipAssociated`), and no other ship salvages any of its parts, so a player who salvages a wreck keeps it. A loose resource is a nugget that is not a tech crate and not the item of a hand-over in a running mission (requirement 1100). `Scavenge.Enabled` (default on) turns scavenging off; then no ship scavenges and no ages are kept.
1331. **Cooldown.** A target counts only `CooldownSeconds` (90) game seconds after the age check first saw it, so a player who just won a fight or cut a wreck loose never sees a scavenger take it. The validation refuses less than 60. Nothing shows the cooldown.
1332. **Age check.** The check runs in the sim tick, every `CheckSeconds` (2) game seconds (a whole number of ticks, at least 1). It walks the live ships and nuggets in sim order. It notes the run game seconds at which it first saw each junk piece and loose resource, and it forgets the ones that are gone. A piece that goes into stasis or leaves with a jump starts from zero when it is live again. A piece that breaks off a wreck is a new junk piece, so its cooldown starts when it breaks off. The ages are run state (`RunState.Scavenge`), so a save, a load and a co-op resync keep them, and the co-op state hash compares their count.
1333. **Pick.** A scavenger picks the nearest target (edge distance, ties by ID) within `SearchRange` (4,000): wrecks on the radar of its team, loose resources in the sight of its team. A loose resource needs room for its type in the storage bays; any target needs storage bays that are not full. A ship without storage bays never scavenges.
1334. **Salvage.** On a wreck, the scavenger holds beside its work part with the placement of requirement 1070: on the part's side of the wreck, with a mining laser aimed at the part within its reach, else with the hull `WorkDistance` (80) outside the wreck's bounding circle. Within 3 × `WorkDistance` it gives the vanilla salvage order for the nearest parts that drop resources, at most `SalvageJobs` (4) open orders at a time, with every resource type marked for collection. Its crew and its mining lasers cut the parts, and the crew carry what comes off to the storage bays.
1335. **Collect.** On a loose resource, the scavenger flies until the resource is within half the crew safe distance (`LoseHomeShipWarningDistance`) of its hull. Then its crew fetch every ripe loose resource within the safe distance (the vanilla collect order).
1336. **Sell.** A scavenger with storage bays at `FullFraction` (0.9) or more stops collecting and sells. One with cargo and no target sells too. It flies to the nearest station that trades with it (live or in stasis), stops `Autonomy.DockDistance` (150) from the hull, and in the vanilla trade distance sells all storage bay cargo there at once (the autonomy sale, requirement 1073). An NPC ship gets no money: the cargo goes into the station stock. A ship that does not sell (`Sells = false`) keeps what it collects and stops collecting when full.
1337. **When.** The `HcScavenge` AI strategy module runs the behavior. It acts only when the ship is idle: no enemy in its sight circle (where the vanilla aggro module picks targets; an enemy only on radar does not count), no attack order, a working command part, and its exit module does not run. Otherwise, with nothing to do, or with scavenging off, it gives control back to the other modules of the AI. Other code can use the same steps: `Scavenger.FindTarget`, `Scavenger.Work` and `Scavenger.Sell`.
1338. **Co-op.** The age check and the module read sim state only and change it only in the sim tick, in sim order, so every machine scavenges the same. The module state (its step) is in the AI integrity hash. The co-op step `scavenge.collect` (`tests/autotest-mp/coop-autonomy.txt`) checks a scavenger across two machines.
1339. **Tests.** `scavenge-basic` (single player): a crewed NPC trade ship with the module leaves a fresh wreck and fresh loose steel alone for the cooldown, then salvages and collects, and sells at the station. Unit tests: `ScavengeModelTests`. Screenshot script: `tests/screens/scavenge.txt`.

### The Scavengers faction

1340. **Faction.** The scavengers have their own player index (`Scavengers.PlayerIndex`, 900) and team. No vanilla faction uses it; the first run start checks that. The vanilla enemy rule makes every other team an enemy; a patch on `CareerSimModeManager.AreEnemies` keeps the scavengers friendly to the players (until provoked, requirement 1341), to the ships of the vanilla factions (military and civilian) and to neutral ships. Pirates, hunters and other hostile sides stay enemies of the scavengers.
1341. **Provocation.** A player can attack a scavenger (vanilla allows an attack order on any non-player ship). When a player's shot hits one (vanilla `CheckIfWarTriggered`; forgivable damage such as spread fire does not count), all scavengers are enemies of the players for `ProvokedSeconds` (300) game seconds, and each later hit starts that time again. A chat line from the hit ship says so. The time is run state, saved and in the co-op hash. When it ends, both sides drop the attack orders and turret targets that they have on each other, and a chat line says that the scavengers stop holding a grudge.
1342. **Ship.** No vanilla built-in ship has a mining laser, so the scavenger is a design that the mod builds from vanilla parts (`ScavengerShip`): two small mining lasers and one small laser blaster at the nose, a small control room, four small crew quarters, a small reactor, two storage bays and small thrusters around a corridor spine, with the game's automatic doors. It arrives with crew, power and empty storage bays, the `HotCargoScavenger` AI and a name "Scavenger N" that no other scavenger in the system has.
1343. **Arrivals.** Every `SpawnCheckSeconds` (60) game seconds, while `FactionEnabled` (default on) and the run is not over, a system with fewer than `MaxPerSystem` (2) scavengers (live, or in the vanilla stasis far from the players) gets one with chance `SpawnChance` (0.35). It arrives `SpawnDistanceMin` to `SpawnDistanceMax` (3,000 to 7,000) from the players' first ship, clear of suns and at least `SpawnDistanceMin` from every station (the hunter arrival rule), facing the players. The roll uses the sim random source in the sim tick.
1344. **Fleeing.** The `HcShy` AI module runs first. Threats are enemies within `FleeRange` (3,000, edge distance). A threat comes the scavenger's way when it flies toward it at `ApproachSpeed` (15) or faster, or has an attack order on it. Then the scavenger flees straight away from the nearest threat, unless it is clearly stronger: its firepower is at least `StrongerRatio` (2) times the firepower of all threats (an unarmed threat never makes it flee). It stops once no threat comes its way and the nearest threat is `SafeDistance` (2,500) away.
1345. **AI.** The `HotCargoScavenger` AI runs, highest first: flee (requirement 1344), scavenge (requirement 1337, selling at stations), the vanilla exit, the vanilla fight against enemies in sight (when it did not flee), the vanilla self repair, and the vanilla area patrol (radius 30,000), so a scavenger with nothing to do wanders the system. Out of the live area it is a vanilla stasis ship.
1346. **Tests.** `scavengers-faction` (single player): arrivals up to the cap, the ship (lasers, crew, empty bays), the relations, salvage of an old wreck, provocation and its end, an enemy pirate, and fleeing. Unit tests: `ScavengeModelTests`.
