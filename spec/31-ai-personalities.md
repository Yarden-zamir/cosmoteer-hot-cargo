## 31. AI personalities

Every hostile AI fights with a personality: a weighted mix of tactics, set per faction, varied per ship and fitted to the ship's build. Tunables: `Tactics`. Pure math: `Ai/PersonalityModel.cs`. Code: `Ai/PersonalityStrategy.cs` (`HcPersonality`), `Tactics/TeamStrategy.cs` (`HcTeam`), `Tactics/RoleTactic.cs` (`HcRole`), `Tactics/CoverStrategy.cs` (`HcCover`).

360. **Scope.** The tactics of requirements 44 to 48, the behaviors of requirements 193 to 202 and the new tactics of requirements 364 to 367 run in the hunter AI (corporate hunters, shady trap and event ambushers) and in the vanilla combat AIs Normal (pirates, bounties, ambushes, faction military), Wanderer, Easy, StationGuard and Police. They act only while the ship is hostile to at least one human player (police and faction ships when the players are wanted) and has not surrendered. Friendly and neutral ships keep the vanilla AI. Defense platforms, military stations, space stations and trade ships keep the vanilla AI: they do not move or do not fight. The Easy AI gets no Disable, because it has no targeting modules. With `PersonalitiesEnabled` off, hunters use every tactic (requirements 43 to 48) and the vanilla AIs use none. The behaviors of requirements 193 to 202 keep their own switches.
361. **Personalities.** `Personalities` lists the personalities. Each has a `Name`, `Groups`, a `Chance`, a `Jitter` and a weight from 0 to 1 for each trait: `Kite`, `Pincer`, `Disable`, `FallBack`, `Bait`, `Team`, `Cover`, `Brawl` and `Call`. A ship's group is `hunters` for the hunter AI, else its faction ID (`monolith`, `cabal`, `imperium`, `fringe`, `io`), else `pirates`. A ship rolls one personality of its group by `Chance`, among those not named in `PersonalitiesOff`. Each trait weight then moves by up to ± `Jitter` (default 0.3), clamped to 0 to 1. The roll and the jitter come from a hash of the ship ID, so two ships of one faction differ, and a ship keeps its personality after a load. A ship of a group without a personality uses no trait. With personalities on, only ships with the `Call` trait call for help (requirement 196). Every ship can answer. Defaults (two per group, the first at chance 2, the second at 1) and their strongest traits:

     | Group | Chance 2 | Chance 1 |
     |---|---|---|
     | monolith | Monolith Phalanx: team, brawl, call | Monolith Lancers: team, kite, disable |
     | cabal | Cabal Zealots: brawl, pincer | Cabal Inquisitors: disable, pincer |
     | imperium | Imperial Line: team, call, fall back, kite | Imperial Hussars: pincer, bait, kite |
     | fringe | Fringe Skirmishers: kite, cover, fall back, bait | Fringe Scrappers: brawl, cover, fall back |
     | io | Io Disruptors: disable, kite, cover | Io Swarm: pincer, team, bait |
     | pirates | Pirate Raiders: fall back, pincer, bait, cover | Pirate Ambushers: cover, fall back, kite |
     | hunters | Corporate Hunters: call, disable, pincer, team | Corporate Sweepers: team, call, brawl |

362. **Build profile.** `HcPersonality` measures the ship in the AI tick, again after `ProfileRefreshSeconds` (default 10 s), and saves the numbers with the ship: acceleration (thrust of the working thrusters ÷ mass), turn (summed thruster torque ÷ rotational inertia), the longest active weapon range, the armor share of the part cells, the shield parts, the bounding radius and the side profile (requirement 1030). Each number maps to a score from 0 to 1 between its two ends in `Profile`: agility, turn, range, protection (the higher of the armor score and the score of shield parts per unit of radius) and size. Agility 0 (acceleration at or below `AccelHeavy`, default 20) marks a heavy ship. The defaults come from a census of the built-in combat ships (autotest command `aicensus`).
363. **Build fit.** A trait is on when its jittered weight × its build fit is at least `PersonalityThreshold` (default 0.45). Fits, from the build scores: Kite = (0.5 + 0.5 agility)(0.3 + 0.7 range). Bait = agility (1 − 0.5 size). Pincer = 0.3 + 0.35 (agility + turn). Brawl = (1 − range)(0.5 + 0.5 protection). Cover = (1 − 0.7 protection)(1 − 0.6 size). Disable, FallBack, Team and Call = 1. A heavy ship never kites or baits. A ship with a weak back never kites, and a ship with no guarded side never baits (requirement 1032). Brawl excludes Kite and Cover: the higher score wins. So fast light ships kite, bait and flank, long-range ships hold distance, short-range protected ships brawl, and unprotected small ships use cover.
364. **Team.** Allies with the Team trait within `TeamRadius` (default 2,500) of a ship form its team, ordered by ID. The member with the lowest ID leads. The team target is the leader's attack target while it is an enemy on the team radar, else the nearest enemy human ship on the leader's radar. Every member attacks the team target, so the team focuses one ship until it dies or leaves radar. A team needs two members.
365. **Roles.** Each team member gets a role from its build scores. The tank (the highest protection + size) closes to `BrawlRangeFraction` (0.5) of its range. The sniper (the longest range among the others, with a range score of 0.5 or more) holds at `KiteRangeFraction` (0.9) of its range. Other members with agility 0.5 or more flank from `TeamFlankDegrees` (90°) left and right of the tank's side, in turn. The rest hold the line: the vanilla and pincer modules move them. Ties go to the lower ID.
366. **Cover.** A ship with the Cover trait whose target is within `CoverEngageRange` (default 1,500, edge to edge) fights for `CoverPeekSeconds` (8 s), then hides for `CoverHideSeconds` (6 s), and repeats. To hide, it flies to the far side of a rock from its target, `CoverGap` (30) clear of the rock. Rocks are junk ships whose rules block weapon fire (`JunkBlocksWeaponLOS`: asteroids and megaroids) within `CoverSearchRange` (2,000) of the ship, with a radius of at least `CoverMinRockFactor` (0.5) × the ship's radius, and not next to the target. The ship picks the rock with the nearest hiding spot. Without a rock, it fights on.
367. **Brawl.** A ship with the Brawl trait and no team role closes to `BrawlRangeFraction` of its longest weapon range, edge to edge.
368. **Tells.** The players read the enemy plan only from how the ships move. A team turns on one ship together: its tank closes in, its sniper hangs back and its flankers swing wide. A covering ship ducks behind a rock. No marker, label, mouseover or log line names a personality, trait, team role, cover, regroup or flight. Only player-facing threats show text: the call progress (requirement 197) and the jump countdown of a fleeing corporate hunter (requirement 195). Debug logging (`Debug.TacticsLog`) still names every decision.
369. **Determinism and switches.** Personality rolls and jitter hash the ship ID. The build profile with its side profile, the team role, leader and target, and the cover phase, rock and target are module state: saved with the ship and part of the integrity hash. Team members and rocks are sorted by ID. All decisions run in the AI tick. Each tactic has `<Tactic>Enabled` (new: `TeamEnabled`, `CoverEnabled`, `BrawlEnabled`). `PersonalitiesOff` switches personalities off by name. Every number is a live tunable. The co-op step `ai.personalities` (`tests/autotest-mp/coop-combat.txt`) fights pirates, Monolith ships and strike hunters among rocks on two machines with no desync.

### Weak sides

Code: `Ai/PersonalityStrategy.cs` (`MeasureSides`, `MoveGuarded`), `Hunters/HunterOrders.cs` (`MoveFacing`). Pure math: `Ai/PersonalityModel.cs` (`Sides`, `RayCover`).

1030. **Side profile.** With the build profile (requirement 362), `HcPersonality` measures the cover of each protected part in 8 ship-local directions, 45° apart. Protected parts have a part category in `VulnerableParts` (default control rooms `command`, reactors `reactor` and the tech vault). The cover of a part in a direction is the sum over the cells on a straight line from the part center to the hull edge: an armor cell gives `ArmorCoverWeight` (default 3), another part cell gives 1, each × the part's health fraction. A direction is weak when a protected part has less cover than `WeakSideCover` (default 4) toward it. A ship without protected parts has no weak side.
1031. **Strong side.** The strong side is the direction with the best score: its lowest protected-part cover, capped at 2 × `WeakSideCover`, + `WeaponSideWeight` (default 1) × the operational weapons that can fire toward it (`Weapon.IsInCoverage` at 0.8 × the weapon range). Ties go to the direction nearest the flight direction.
1032. **Fit.** A ship whose back (opposite its flight direction) is weak never kites: the vanilla attack command turns the ship's front to its destination when it backs off far (`BaseFollowCommand.GetCurrentIntendedRotation`), which shows the back to the target. A ship with no guarded side (every side weak) never baits. Both rules hold with personalities off too.
1033. **Guarded moves.** Flee, fall back, regroup, both bait phases and the cover hide move use one guarded move. A plain move turns the ship's front to the destination (`MoveCommand.GetCurrentIntendedRotation`). When that would point a weak side at the threat (the nearest enemy, the bait or cover target, or the regroup target), the ship keeps its strong side toward the threat and strafes, like `AIAggroEnemiesModule.TacticalRetreat`: a move with forced rotation and the threat as its rotation target.
1034. **Switch, cost and determinism.** `WeakSideEnabled` switches requirements 1030 to 1033 off. The side profile is measured only on the build profile refresh (`ProfileRefreshSeconds`), in the AI tick, saved with the ship and part of the integrity hash. The autotest `ai-weakside` checks that a ship with an exposed control room at its back keeps its strong side to the enemy while it flees.
